01 · Mood & readiness

The room got more confident, not more nervous

We asked the same question at the start and end of the session. Enthusiasm grew over the hour and worry stayed low. Of the phones that answered both times, moved toward embracing AI and moved away from it.

02 · Where we agree

AI drafts. People verify and decide.

Whenever a scenario put AI in front of members or put a decision about a person on the line, the room chose the option that kept a human in charge. This is the strongest finding of the session.

Share choosing the human-in-the-loop answer

Hover or tap a bar for the full question and answer.

Right-sized tools, too. Given a simple, low-stakes task (rewriting a short member webinar announcement), chose a fast, low-cost AI model over a more powerful one.
03 · Gray zones

Where pool staff need clearer rules

Staff rated four requests from the fictional member city of Riscville as green (normal work), yellow (add safeguards) or red (stop or reframe). The splits show where well-meaning people make different calls on the same request.

Green, yellow or red?

The outlined segment is the answer the session was designed to teach.

  • Green: normal work
  • Yellow: add safeguards
  • Red: stop or reframe
Risk by department, not by data. Writing interview questions from a public job posting was meant to be an easy green, yet of the room slowed it to yellow. The word "HR" did the work, even though nothing sensitive was involved.
04 · Policy votes

The room's AI policy, one question at a time

The final part of the session asked the room to build an AI policy together. Most answers point to a clear consensus. Sensitive data and member disclosure are the two to settle deliberately.

How the room voted

Each bar is one question. Options are listed under each bar.

A note on disclosure. The AI-generated summary shown live in the session described the room as mostly favoring disclosure for significant content. The actual vote went the other way: said no disclosure is needed when a person has reviewed the work. The chart above shows the real numbers.
05 · Real dilemmas

What pool staff are actually asking

Attendees submitted the real AI questions they face at work, anonymously and as typed. Most touch personal information, employment decisions, claims data or rating, and two suggest staff are already experimenting without an approved tool.

    About the data

    • Collected live during the "AI Control Room: Leveraging AI Without Scaring Everyone" session at the NLC-RISC Staff Conference on October 27, 2026.
    • Voting was anonymous. No names, emails or organizations were collected, and attendees were asked not to enter sensitive information.
    • Percentages count one vote per phone per question, using each phone's final answer, so they may differ slightly from the live totals shown in the room. Between 31 and 49 phones answered each question.
    • Attendees chose to be in the room. Treat these results as a snapshot of engaged pool staff, not a statistical survey of all NLC-RISC pools.
    • The prompt-writing exercise and the live bonus-round vote are not included because there were too few usable responses.
