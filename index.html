02 · Where we agree

AI drafts. People verify and decide.

Whenever a scenario put AI in front of members or put a decision about a person on the line, the room chose the option that kept a human in charge. This is the strongest finding of the session.

Right-sized tools, too. Given a simple, low-stakes task (rewriting a short member webinar announcement), chose a fast, low-cost AI model over a more powerful one.

Where pool staff need clearer rules

Staff rated four requests from the fictional member city of Riscville as green (normal work), yellow (add safeguards) or red (stop or reframe). The splits show where well-meaning people make different calls on the same request.

04 · Policy votes

The room's AI policy, one question at a time

The final part of the session asked the room to build an AI policy together. Most answers point to a clear consensus. Sensitive data and member disclosure are the two to settle deliberately.

05 · Real dilemmas

What pool staff are actually asking

Attendees submitted the real AI questions they face at work, anonymously and as typed. Most touch personal information, employment decisions, claims data or rating, and two suggest staff are already experimenting without an approved tool.

    06 · Pool websites

    Members can find the page. Then they get stuck.

    In a session on improving pool websites, attendees scored pages from their own pool's site against the FIND framework: is the page Findable, Intuitive, Necessary and Directed toward a clear next step? Each "no" or "not sure" adds a point, so higher scores mean more problems.

    The average page scored out of 4, and failed three or more of the four tests. The most common failure was a missing next step: pages that don't tell members what to do.

    Why this matters for AI. Members increasingly ask AI tools their questions instead of browsing pool websites, and those tools read our pages to answer. Clear, current pages with an obvious next step get summarized correctly. Cluttered ones get misread or skipped.

    About the data

    • Sections 01 to 05 come from an interactive session on leveraging AI, held at a national pool staff conference in October 2026. Section 06 comes from a session on using AI to improve pool websites, held at a national pool conference in early October 2026.
    • Voting was anonymous. No names, emails or organizations were collected, and attendees were asked not to enter sensitive information.
    • AI session percentages count one vote per phone per question, using each phone's final answer, so they may differ slightly from the live totals shown in the room. Between 31 and 49 phones answered each question.
    • Website audits were scored by attendees on pages from their own pool's website and shared anonymously (page type, score and flagged issues only, no URLs).
    • Attendees chose to be in the room. Treat these results as a snapshot of engaged pool staff, not a statistical survey of the industry.
    • The prompt-writing exercise and the live bonus-round vote are not included because there were too few usable responses.
